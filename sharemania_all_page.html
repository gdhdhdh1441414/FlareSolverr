    Lil Suzy
    Take Me In Your Arms    ​

    vicky lived across the street from us. she had a two parent house hold and went to summer camp. so naturally, her parents didn’t want her hanging with the likes of me.

    vicky was puerto rican and idolized madonna. she dyed her hair blonde, had a mole above her lip - which even now i am not sure if it was real or not. and like madonna, vicky surrounded herself with a group of gay men, many of whom were not even “out” at the time. but that is a story for another day.

    in the summer of 1992, i wasn’t the biggest fan of freestyle music. so when i saw vicky sitting on her steps with a big boombox blasting “take me in your arms,” i rolled my eyes and probably said some shit like, “she loves that damn music!” vicky waved and i walked over to chat with her. as we talked, she would rewind the cassette and replay “take me in your arms.” i wanted to say, “gurl! please!” but by the third listen, i was singing along. “this song has the ‘planet rock’ beat.” she nodded her head.

    “take me in your arms” was not the first freestyle song to sample the afrika bambaataa and the soul sonic force 1982 single but it was the most successful up until that point and would inspire million imitations. (i think Nyasia's "now & forever [florida hip-hip mix] may have been one of the first).

    lil suzy released her first single “randy” in 1988 when she was just eight years old. The bubblegum dance/pop song garnered some buzz but went relatively unnoticed by radio. her fortunes would turn a few years later with the release of the double A-side single “take me in your arms/love can’t wait.” the label could not decide between the two songs as the lead single from suzy’s debut album so they released both.

    officially released in november 1991, “take me in your arms” proved be the break-out record and became a crossover radio hit in the summer of 1992 reaching number 67 on billboard’s hot 100 that august. “falling in love” would serve as her follow-up single but with freestyle music being pulled from radio stations, the song just could not have the same impact as “take me…”

    vicky and i remained friends for the next few years. she moved off the block and we saw each other less frequently. she passed away in the late 90s not too long after i began dancing for lil suzy.

    more than 30 years since that summer afternoon on vicky’s steps, i have become not only a freestyle music fan but a freestyle advocate and historian. i still remember vicky and now my son knows every word of “take me in your arms.”

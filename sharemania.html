    "Love Never Felt So Good" is a song by American singer and songwriter Michael Jackson, released posthumously on May 2, 2014, as the first single from his second posthumous album, Xscape. The track was originally conceived and recorded in 1980 during a collaborative session between Jackson and Canadian singer-songwriter Paul Anka, around the same time as "This Is It" and "Don't Matter to Me". The original demo featured only Jackson's vocals, finger snaps, and a piano played by Anka. It was later recorded by Johnny Mathis with revised lyrics by Anka and Kathleen Wakefield for his 1984 album, A Special Part of Me.

    In 2010, co-executor of Jackson's estate, John McClain, and Dutch composer Giorgio Tuinfort remixed the track for consideration on the album Michael, which was ultimately not selected. The McClain/Tuinfort version was later chosen as the lead track for the Xscape album. A second version, a duet featuring American singer Justin Timberlake, was produced by Timbaland and J-Roc and included as a bonus track on the deluxe edition of Xscape. This duet version incorporates sampled percussion and breaths from Jackson's 1979 song "Working Day and Night".

    The duet version features Michael Jackson and Justin Timberlake, with Timberlake contributing vocals and directing the music video alongside Rich Lee. The video premiered on May 14, 2014, on The Ellen DeGeneres Show and features Timberlake and a group of young dancers performing iconic Jackson dance moves, interspersed with archival footage of Jackson's music videos and live performances. The duet version received a nomination for Best Choreography at the 2014 MTV Video Music Awards.

    The song was released digitally on May 2, 2014, following a premiere at the iHeartRadio Music Awards on May 1, 2014, where it was introduced with a tribute by Usher. It debuted on the US Billboard Hot 100 at No. 20 and reached No. 9 on May 31, 2014, making Jackson the first artist in history to have a top 10 single on the Billboard Hot 100 in five different decades (six if his Jackson 5 tenure is included). The duet version became Justin Timberlake's 16th top-ten single on the US Billboard Hot 100. The song also spent eight weeks at number one on the US Adult R&B Songs chart and reached number 8 on the UK Singles Chart. It charted within the top ten in eighteen international charts, reaching number one in South Korea, Denmark, Israel, South Africa, and Germany, as well as Belgium's urban chart.

    Critics offered generally positive reviews. Aisha Harris of Slate magazine praised the duet version, stating it "sounds like an outtake from Off the Wall". Carl Willott of Idolator noted the "uncannily natural combination" of Jackson and Timberlake's voices. While some critics, like Chris Richards of The Washington Post, were less enthusiastic about the Timberlake remix, calling it reminiscent of the 2010 album Michael, others, like Gavin Edwards of Rolling Stone, felt it could have easily been a single from Jackson's Bad album.

    "Don't Give Up" is a song written by English rock musician Peter Gabriel and recorded as a duet with English singer Kate Bush for Gabriel's fifth solo studio album, So, released in 1986. The song was inspired by the Depression-era photographs of Dorothea Lange, which depicted poverty-stricken Americans in Dust Bowl conditions, and also by the high unemployment rates in the United Kingdom during the premiership of Margaret Thatcher. Gabriel composed the lyrics about a man whose unemployment causes stress in his domestic relationship, with the verses sung by Gabriel reflecting his feelings of isolation and despair, while the choruses, sung by Bush, offer words of hope and encouragement. The title "Don't Give Up" was a phrase Gabriel's former wife, Jill, would use when he was experiencing emotional difficulties, and he noted that the song was very much about their relationship.

    The song was initially conceived as a solo effort by Gabriel, but he later decided that a duet with a woman would better convey the narrative of a man and woman facing hardship together. Gabriel originally approached country singer Dolly Parton to sing the female part, but she declined, leading to Kate Bush taking the role. Bush was initially nervous but delivered an "excellent" vocal take on her second attempt, with Gabriel and assistant engineer David Stallbaumer noting her contribution significantly improved the song. The recording sessions took place in 1985, and the album version of the song is 6 minutes long, while the 12-inch single edit is 6 minutes and the 7-inch single edit is 5 minutes.

    An edited version of the song was released as the third single from the album in the UK on 20 October 1986 and as the fourth single in the US in March 1987. It spent 11 weeks on the UK singles chart in 1986, peaking at number nine. In 1987, the song won an Ivor Novello Award for Best Song Musically and Lyrically. The song achieved chart success in several countries, reaching number four in the Netherlands and Ireland, number five in Australia, number seven in the Vlaamse Radio 2 Top 30, number nine in the UK and Vlaamse Ultratop 50, and number 13 in Spain. It also reached number 16 in New Zealand, number 23 in the TROS Europarade, number 27 in Germany, number 40 in Canada, and number 72 in the United States.

    Two music videos were created for the song. The first, directed by Godley & Creme, is a single take of Gabriel and Bush embracing as they sing, with the sun behind them entering a total eclipse and re-emerging. Gabriel recalled that the idea of the embrace was suggested by the directors, and he sought his wife Jill's blessing before filming, though she found the video difficult to watch. The second video, directed by Jim Blashfield and produced by Melissa Marsland in 1988, features the singers' faces superimposed over footage of a town and its people in disrepair.
